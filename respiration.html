AQA 4.4.2.1

1. Aerobic & anaerobic respiration

AQA describes cellular respiration as an exothermic reaction. It occurs continuously in living cells and transfers energy for living processes.
Aerobic: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water
C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O
FeatureAerobic respirationAnaerobic respiration in muscles
OxygenUses oxygen.Does not use oxygen.
GlucoseComplete oxidation.Incomplete oxidation.
ProductsCarbon dioxide and water.Lactic acid.
Energy transferred per glucose moleculeMuch more.Much less.
LocationMainly in mitochondria.In the cytoplasm.
Anaerobic in muscles: glucose → lactic acid
  • Movement: energy is needed for muscle contraction.
  • Building larger molecules: for example, joining amino acids to synthesise proteins.
  • Keeping warm: energy transfer helps mammals and birds maintain body temperature.
  • Active transport also requires energy from respiration; give the named process if the question asks about a particular cell.
  • Respiration is a chemical process in cells; breathing is ventilation. Plants respire as well as photosynthesise.
Exam answer essentials
  • Use energy released / transferred, not “energy made, produced or created”. (2021 Higher Q04.4; 2022 Higher Q06.4.)
  • Compare both pathways using matched points. For muscles, anaerobic respiration does not produce carbon dioxide.
AQA 4.4.2.2

2. Response to exercise

  • During exercise, muscles contract more and need more energy. Their respiration rate increases.
  • Heart rate increases → more oxygenated blood reaches the muscles per minute. Blood also supplies glucose and removes carbon dioxide.
  • Breathing rate and breath volume increase → more air is ventilated → oxygen enters the blood and carbon dioxide is removed faster.
  • Link oxygen supply to more aerobic respiration → more energy released for muscle contraction. “The body needs more air” is too vague.
  • When oxygen supply cannot meet demand, anaerobic respiration contributes additional energy transfer. Aerobic respiration does not necessarily stop.
Exam answer essentials
  • Name the change and its purpose. Breathing rate is breaths per minute; breath volume is the amount of air moved per breath.
  • For an exercise investigation, compare the same person at rest and after a standardised activity; keep duration/intensity and measurement intervals consistent. Use repeats, allow recovery and avoid unsafe exertion.
AQA 4.4.2.1

3. Anaerobic respiration in plants & yeast

Glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide
  • Plant and yeast cells have a different anaerobic pathway from human muscles: the products are ethanol and carbon dioxide.
  • Fermentation is the name for anaerobic respiration in yeast. It has economic importance in making bread and alcoholic drinks.
  • In bread dough, yeast breaks down sugars and releases carbon dioxide, making the dough rise.
  • For alcoholic drinks, ethanol is the useful product. Conditions must allow yeast enzymes to function.
  • Anaerobic pathways transfer much less energy per glucose molecule because oxidation of glucose is incomplete.
Exam answer essentials
  • Match products to the organism: muscles → lactic acid; plants/yeast → ethanol + carbon dioxide.
  • When yeast data are supplied, use temperature, sugar availability and oxygen conditions from the question. Do not assume all yeast respiration is anaerobic.

4. Muscle fatigue, oxygen debt & the liver

Oxygen debt: the extra oxygen needed after exercise to react with and remove accumulated lactic acid from cells. This follows AQA’s GCSE explanation; HT only for the detailed definition and liver role.
  1. Insufficient oxygen supply during vigorous activity → anaerobic respiration in muscles.
  2. Glucose is incompletely oxidised → lactic acid accumulates and an oxygen debt develops.
  3. During prolonged vigorous activity, muscles become fatigued and contract less efficiently.
  4. After exercise, breathing and heart rate remain raised for a time, supplying extra oxygen as the body recovers.
  5. HT only: blood carries lactic acid from muscles to the liver, where it is converted back into glucose.
Exam answer essentials
  • Do not define oxygen debt merely as “being out of breath” or oxygen missing from the lungs. Link it to extra oxygen after exercise and accumulated lactic acid.
  • Keep the stages in order: insufficient oxygen → anaerobic respiration → lactic acid → fatigue / oxygen debt → recovery.

5. Metabolism: building & breaking down molecules

Metabolism, using AQA’s wording: “the sum of all the reactions in a cell or the body.”
ProcessWhat happens / why it matters
Carbohydrate synthesisGlucose is converted to starch in plants, glycogen in animals, or cellulose for plant cell walls.
Lipid synthesisOne glycerol molecule combines with three fatty-acid molecules to form a lipid molecule.
Protein synthesisPlants use glucose and nitrate ions to form amino acids; amino acids are joined to synthesise proteins.
RespirationBreaks down glucose and transfers energy for other metabolic processes.
Excess protein breakdownExcess proteins are broken down to form urea, which is excreted.
  • Metabolism includes both synthesis and breakdown, not just digestion or respiration. Enzymes control these reactions.
  • Energy transferred by respiration supports continual enzyme-controlled processes that synthesise new molecules.
  • For a small mammal losing heat rapidly: greater surface area relative to volume → faster heat loss → more respiration needed to help maintain temperature.
Exam answer essentials
  • If asked what energy is used for, give a named process, such as protein synthesis or muscle contraction. “Growth” or “metabolism” without explanation may be too vague. (2021 Higher Q04.3.)
  • For the heat-loss application, compare surface area to volume ratio, not just absolute size. (2022 Higher Q06.4.)
