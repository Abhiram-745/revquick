AQA 4.1.1.1

1. Eukaryotes & prokaryotes

  • Eukaryotic cells have genetic material enclosed in a nucleus.
  • Animal and plant cells are eukaryotic.
  • Prokaryotic cells, such as bacteria, have genetic material that is not enclosed in a nucleus.
Exam answer essentials
  • Compare the same feature on both sides: “Bacterial DNA is not enclosed in a nucleus, whereas animal-cell DNA is.” Do not claim that bacteria have no DNA or no cell membrane.
  • Use the features shown in an unfamiliar cell image rather than assuming every eukaryote has an animal-cell structure.
FeatureAnimal / plant cellsBacterial cells
Genetic materialDNA in a nucleusSingle DNA loop free in cytoplasm; may also contain small circular plasmids
Shared structuresCell membrane, cytoplasm and ribosomesCell membrane, cytoplasm and ribosomes
Other structuresMitochondria; photosynthetic plant cells also have chloroplastsCell wall; no mitochondria or chloroplasts
Relative sizeUsually much largerMuch smaller than eukaryotic cells

2. Sub-cellular structures

Exam answer essentials
  • The membrane controls entry and exit; the wall supports the cell.
  • Say energy is released by respiration, not created by mitochondria.
  • Ribosomes make proteins, not energy.
  • Each structure contributes to a cell’s function.
  • Learn a precise function for each one, then use it to explain unfamiliar cells.
StructureFunction you should know
NucleusContains genetic material and controls the cell’s activities.
CytoplasmWhere most chemical reactions occur; enzymes control these reactions.
Cell membraneControls the movement of substances into and out of the cell.
MitochondriaSite of aerobic respiration, which releases energy for cellular processes.
RibosomesSite of protein synthesis.
Cell wallMade of cellulose in plants and algae; strengthens and supports the cell.
ChloroplastsSite of photosynthesis; contain chlorophyll, which absorbs light energy.
Permanent vacuoleContains cell sap; helps maintain turgor and support the plant cell.
Plasmids in bacteriaSmall circular DNA molecules carrying additional genes, which may provide useful characteristics such as antibiotic resistance.
  • Most animal cells have the first five structures.
  • Plant cells have these too, plus a cellulose cell wall and permanent vacuole; photosynthetic plant cells also have chloroplasts.
  • Root hair cells normally lack chloroplasts because they are underground.
  • Bacterial cell walls are not made of cellulose; cellulose walls are a plant and algal feature.
AQA 4.1.1.3

3. Specialised cells

A specialised cell has structures adapted to carry out a particular function.
Exam answer essentials
  • Use feature → effect → function: “The root hair gives a large surface area, increasing absorption of water and mineral ions.” Naming a feature alone may not earn an explanation mark.
  • For mitochondria, state aerobic respiration releases energy and name its use: tail movement, muscle contraction or active transport. Do not say mitochondria “make energy”.
Cell & functionAdaptation → why it helps
Sperm: fertilise an egg
  • Tail (flagellum) → propels the sperm towards the egg.
  • Many mitochondria → aerobic respiration releases energy for movement of the tail.
  • Acrosome in the head contains enzymes → digests the egg’s outer layers so the sperm can penetrate.
  • Haploid nucleus → contains one set of chromosomes, which combines with the egg’s set at fertilisation.
Nerve: transmit electrical impulses
  • Long axon → carries electrical impulses over long distances.
  • Branched ends → connect the nerve cell to other cells.
  • Myelin sheath insulates the axon → increases the speed of electrical-impulse transmission.
Muscle: contract for movement
  • Contractile protein fibres → shorten to cause contraction.
  • Many mitochondria → aerobic respiration releases energy for contraction.
Root hair: absorb water and mineral ions
  • Long hair-like projection → a large surface area for absorption.
  • Water enters by osmosis; mineral ions can be absorbed against their concentration gradient by active transport.
  • Many mitochondria → respiration releases the energy needed for active transport of mineral ions.
Xylem: carry water and mineral ions; support the plant
  • Dead, hollow cells with no cytoplasm and no end walls between adjacent cells → form continuous tubes for water and mineral-ion transport.
  • Walls strengthened with lignin → support the plant and prevent the tubes collapsing.
Phloem: transport dissolved sugars
  • Elongated living cells form sieve tubes.
  • Pores in end walls (sieve plates) → allow dissolved sugars in cell sap to pass between cells.
  • Companion cells have many mitochondria → respiration releases energy for active transport involved in moving sugars. The transport of dissolved sugars through phloem is translocation.

4. Differentiation

Exam answer essentials
  • Keep the two processes distinct: mitosis increases the number of cells; differentiation produces specialised cells.
Differentiation is the process by which a cell changes to become specialised for its function.

Animals

  • Most types of animal cell differentiate at an early stage.
  • Many mature cells lose the ability to differentiate.
  • In mature animals, cell division mainly replaces and repairs cells.

Plants

  • Many types of plant cell retain the ability to differentiate throughout life.
  • Cells in meristems continue dividing and can produce specialised cells for growth.
  • As cells differentiate, they develop different sub-cellular structures.
  • For example, a muscle cell develops many mitochondria and contractile fibres.
  • This allows it to perform its specific function.

5. Size & units

Exam answer essentials
  • Read the question’s units before calculating.
  • Keep the numerical value and unit together; do not round intermediate steps unnecessarily.
  • You should compare cell and sub-cellular sizes, use standard form, and understand differences in orders of magnitude.
1 cm = 10 mm   ·   1 mm = 1,000 µm   ·   1 µm = 1,000 nm
  • For smaller units, multiply; for larger units, divide.
  • For example, 0.025 mm = 25 µm = 25,000 nm.
  • In standard form, 0.025 mm = 2.5 × 10−2 mm.
  • An order of magnitude is a factor of 10.
  • A 20 µm cell is 10 times the length of a 2 µm bacterium: a difference of one order of magnitude.
  • Typical values are context, not fixed sizes for every cell.
  • When estimating the size of a structure in an image, compare it with a known scale or cell size.
  • If a nucleus is about one-quarter the width of a 40 µm cell, estimate its width as 10 µm.
  • Estimate areas using appropriate shapes, such as πr² for a roughly circular outline; state the simplification.
