1. Lung structure & alveoli

Gas exchange is the movement of oxygen into the blood and carbon dioxide out of it at the lungs, by diffusion.
Exam answer essentials
  • Use adaptation → effect: alveolar and capillary walls are one cell thick → short diffusion distance. Thin walls do not create a large surface area.
  • Air passes through the trachea, then two bronchi, smaller bronchioles and finally the alveoli.
  • Many small alveoli provide a large surface area for gas exchange.
  • Alveolar surfaces are moist so gases can dissolve. The alveolar wall and surrounding capillary wall are thin → a short diffusion distance.
  • Ventilation brings fresh oxygen-rich air; blood flow carries oxygen away and brings carbon dioxide → maintains concentration gradients.
  • Oxygen diffuses from alveolar air into the blood because its concentration is higher in the alveoli. Carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the alveoli because its concentration is higher in the arriving blood.

2. Ventilation: breathing in & out

Ventilation moves air into and out of the lungs. It refreshes alveolar air so diffusion can continue.
Exam answer essentials
  • Explain the full sequence: muscles → chest volume → pressure → air movement. Air moves down a pressure difference; the lungs do not actively pull it in.
StageBreathing inNormal breathing out
DiaphragmContracts and flattens.Relaxes and becomes more domed.
Intercostal muscles / ribsExternal intercostals contract; ribs move up and out.Muscles relax; ribs move down and in.
Thorax volumeIncreases.Decreases.
Pressure in lungsFalls below outside air pressure.Rises above outside air pressure.
Air movementAir enters.Air leaves.
  • The lungs are organs; they are not muscles. Movement of the thorax changes lung volume.
  • Breathing is ventilation; respiration is a chemical process in cells that releases energy. Do not use the terms interchangeably.

3. Inhaled & exhaled air

Exam answer essentials
  • Exhaled air still contains oxygen. Do not claim all oxygen is used, or that nitrogen is produced by respiration.
FeatureInhaled airExhaled air
OxygenAbout 21%.About 16%; less because oxygen enters the blood and is used by cells.
Carbon dioxideAbout 0.04%.About 4%; more because respiring cells produce carbon dioxide.
NitrogenAbout 78%.A similar proportion; the body does not use it directly in respiration.
Water vapour / temperatureVariable with the environment.Usually more water vapour and warmer.
  • The percentages are approximate and depend on conditions; use the values given in an exam question.
  • Exhaled air turns limewater cloudy more quickly because it contains more carbon dioxide. Compare equal air volumes for a fair test.
  • Ventilation maintains gradients; it does not itself carry out the chemical reactions of respiration.
