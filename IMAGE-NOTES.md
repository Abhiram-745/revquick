# Illustration brief

Created using the built-in AI image-generation capability. Direction: original flat 2D educational illustrations, thin navy outlines, pale colours, white backgrounds, typed labels, minimal detail, no photorealism. Inspired by general GCSE textbook visual conventions after researching Save My Exams and Cognito references; no source artwork copied.

## Assets and prompts

1. cells.webp — animal, plant and bacterial cell comparison; main structures. Plant boundary leaders removed after review: outer thick green wall and inner membrane identified in caption. Plant mitochondria and ribosomes omitted intentionally and disclosed.
2. specialisation.webp — six panels: sperm, nerve, muscle, root hair, xylem, phloem; concise adaptation labels.
3. microscope.webp — microscope parts, magnification versus resolution.
4. microscopy-practical.webp — eight numbered steps: epidermis, water, iodine, angled coverslip, low power, coarse focus, fine focus, higher power.
5. culture-practical.webp — six steps: clean, inoculated agar/sterile tools, treatment/control discs, taped lid, inverted incubation ≤25°C, measure zones.
6. cell-transport.webp — diffusion high to low; osmosis water dilute to concentrated through partial membrane; active transport low to high with energy from respiration.
7. osmosis-practical.webp — equal-size tissue, blot/initial mass, concentration range with same time/temp, blot/final mass, repeats and percentage change. Cylinders are schematic: notes require measuring equal dimensions.
8. osmosis-cells.webp — plant and animal cell changes. At equal concentration no net movement is explicitly shown; movement still occurs both ways as described in caption.
9. cell-cycle.webp — growth, DNA copying, nuclear division, two daughter cells; no literal chromosome counting in the picture.
10. stem-cells.webp — embryonic, adult marrow and plant meristem examples. Platelets noted as cell fragments.

Mathematical values, comparison tables and definitions are live HTML text for accuracy and accessibility. Scientific illustrations are simplified visual aids; captions and notes provide exact statements.
