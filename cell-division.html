Cell division & stem cells

Follow the cell cycle, explain mitosis, and compare stem-cell sources, uses and risks.

1. Chromosomes, DNA & genes

The nucleus contains chromosomes, which are made from DNA. Each chromosome carries many genes. In body cells, chromosomes are normally found in pairs.

Nucleus → chromosomes made of DNA → many genes

A gene is a small section of DNA that codes for a protein. Human body cells normally contain 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs; other species have different numbers.

Mark scheme focusKeep the hierarchy correct: genes are sections of DNA on chromosomes. A chromosome does not contain just one gene.
AQA 4.1.2.2

2. The cell cycle & mitosis

Mitosis is cell division in which identical sets of chromosomes are separated into two nuclei, producing genetically identical cells after the cell divides.
  1. Before mitosis: the cell grows and increases the number of sub-cellular structures such as mitochondria and ribosomes.
  2. DNA is replicated: copies of the chromosomes are made.
  3. During mitosis: one set of chromosomes is pulled to each end of the cell and the nucleus divides.
  4. Finally: the cytoplasm and cell membrane divide, producing two daughter cells.

The daughter cells are genetically identical to one another and normally to the parent cell. They have the same number of chromosomes as the original cell.

Mitosis is needed for growth and development, replacement and repair of damaged cells, and asexual reproduction. For example, a body cell with 46 chromosomes produces two daughter cells with 46 each—not 23 each.

Mark scheme focusDNA is copied before mitosis, not after cell division. Explain “identical” in terms of genetic material and chromosome sets. Do not halve the chromosome number in a mitosis answer.

3. Stem-cell sources

A stem cell is an undifferentiated cell that can divide to produce more cells and can differentiate into specialised cell types.
SourceAbility to differentiateUse / importance
Human embryosCan form most types of human cell.Can be cloned and made to differentiate into different cell types.
Adult bone marrowCan form a limited range of cells, including blood cells.Used in treatments such as bone-marrow transplants.
Plant meristemsCan differentiate into any type of plant cell throughout life.Enable growth and can be used to produce clones.
Mark scheme focusDo not say all adult cells are stem cells, or that adult bone-marrow stem cells can form every human cell type. Compare the range of differentiation.

4. Medical uses, cloning & evaluation

Stem cells could replace damaged or non-functioning cells. Potential applications include replacing insulin-producing cells in diabetes or nerve cells after spinal injury causing paralysis. These are potential treatments, not a guarantee of a cure.

Therapeutic cloning: an embryo is produced with the same genes as the patient. Stem cells from it can be used to make replacement cells. For the AQA model, the patient-matched stem cells are not rejected because they have the same genes as the patient.

Evaluate both benefits and concerns

BenefitRisk / concern
Could restore damaged tissues and improve quality of life.Cells may transfer infections, including viruses.
Patient-matched cells reduce rejection.Some people object to using or destroying embryos for ethical or religious reasons.
Can provide cells for research and developing treatments.The success, safety and suitability depend on the treatment; use any evidence given in the question.

An evaluation should connect each point to the question’s scenario and end with a justified judgement. Ethical objections differ between people; explain the concern rather than treating a belief as a biological fact.

Mark scheme focusFor therapeutic cloning, link same genes as the patient → reduced immune rejection. “It produces identical cells” alone does not explain the medical advantage.

5. Meristems & plant cloning

Meristem stem cells can be used to make genetically identical copies of a plant quickly and economically.

Conservation

Rare species can be cloned to protect them from extinction.

Agriculture

Large numbers of crop plants with useful features, such as disease resistance, can be produced.

The useful characteristics are retained because the plants are clones. However, low genetic variation can mean plants share susceptibility to a new disease or environmental change: use this if asked to evaluate a cloning programme.

Mark scheme focusGive a specific useful characteristic and why copying it helps. Plant meristem cells can differentiate throughout life; this differs from most mature animal cells.
