1. Photosynthesis: the reaction

AQA describes photosynthesis as an endothermic reaction: light transfers energy from the environment to chloroplasts.
Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen
6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2
  • Chloroplasts are the site of photosynthesis. They contain chlorophyll, which absorbs light.
  • Carbon dioxide enters leaves through stomata; water is absorbed by roots and transported in xylem.
  • Glucose is made and oxygen is released. The glucose can be used immediately or converted to other substances.
  • Recognise CO₂, H₂O, O₂ and C₆H₁₂O₆. Correct letter cases and subscripts matter.
  • Light and chlorophyll are conditions for the reaction; they are not consumed chemical reactants.
Exam answer essentials
  • Distinguish chloroplast = organelle from chlorophyll = light-absorbing pigment.
  • Energy is transferred by light; photosynthesis does not create energy. Use the equation requested: word equation or chemical symbols.

2. Leaf adaptations & proving photosynthesis

StructureLink to photosynthesis
Broad, thin leafLarge area exposed to light; a short diffusion distance for gases.
Transparent upper surfaceLets light reach photosynthetic cells below.
Palisade cells with many chloroplastsAbsorb more light for photosynthesis.
Stomata and spongy-mesophyll air spacesAllow carbon dioxide to diffuse to photosynthesising cells.
Xylem in veinsSupplies water for the reaction.
PhloemTransports dissolved sugars to where they are used or stored.
  • An iodine test detects starch. Starch production is evidence that photosynthesis has occurred under the tested conditions.
  • Destarch a plant by keeping it in the dark long enough to use stored starch, often around 24–48 hours depending on the plant. This makes newly formed starch easier to identify.
Exam answer essentials
  • Explain structure → function; “leaves have chloroplasts” alone does not explain an adaptation.

3. Rate of photosynthesis & limiting factors

A limiting factor is a factor restricting the rate. Increasing it can raise the rate until a different factor becomes limiting.
FactorEffect and explanation
Light intensityMore light usually increases energy transfer and the rate, until another factor limits it.
Carbon dioxide concentrationMore CO₂ supplies more reactant, raising the rate until another factor limits it.
TemperatureIncreasing temperature towards an optimum increases enzyme-controlled reaction rates. Excessively high temperature can denature enzymes and reduce the rate.
Amount of chlorophyllLess chlorophyll means less light absorbed, so the rate can decrease. Chlorophyll loss can result from magnesium deficiency or disease.

HT: use a comparison to identify the limiting factor

  • HT only: factors interact. Compare curves at the same x-value and see which changed condition increases the rate.
  • On a rising light-intensity curve, light is limiting. At a plateau, another factor is limiting; do not name it with certainty unless the comparison data identify it.
  • If a higher-CO₂ curve gives a higher rate at the same light intensity and temperature, carbon dioxide was limiting the lower curve.
Exam answer essentials
  • Explain the plateau: more of the plotted factor has no effect because a different factor restricts the rate.
  • For an explain question, include the mechanism: light supplies energy; CO₂ is a reactant; temperature affects enzyme activity.

4. Inverse square law & greenhouse economics

Relative light intensity ∝ 1 ÷ distance²
Lamp distanceRelative intensityCompared with 10 cm
10 cm1/10² = 0.01001
20 cm1/20² = 0.0025¼
30 cm1/30² ≈ 0.00111⅑
  • Doubling distance gives one-quarter the intensity; halving distance gives four times the intensity.
  • Use I₂ / I₁ = (d₁ / d₂)². If intensity is 80 arbitrary units at 20 cm, at 40 cm it is 80 × (20/40)² = 20 units.
  • Keep distance units consistent. A value of 1/d² is a relative measure, not automatically a reading in lux.
  • This is an idealised relationship; use it when the question assumes the inverse square law. Photosynthesis rate is not necessarily proportional to intensity after a plateau.
  • Increasing light, heat or carbon dioxide can increase photosynthesis when that factor is limiting → more plant growth / yield.
  • Extra lighting, heating and CO₂ supply cost money. Profit = income − costs.
  • Choose conditions where extra crop income exceeds extra costs. Maximum possible photosynthesis is not necessarily maximum profit.
  • A heater may bring enzymes towards their optimum; overheating can reduce the rate. Increasing a non-limiting factor may waste money.
Exam answer essentials
  • Show the square in a light-intensity calculation. “Twice as far means half the light” is incorrect.
  • For greenhouse evaluation, link limiting factor → extra yield → extra income versus cost.

5. Uses of glucose in plants

  • Respiration: glucose is broken down to release energy for living processes.
  • Starch: glucose is converted into insoluble starch for storage. Insolubility means it does not dissolve and alter the cell’s water concentration like a soluble store would.
  • Fats and oils: made for storage, for example in seeds.
  • Cellulose: made for cell walls, providing strength and supporting growth.
  • Amino acids and proteins: plants use glucose together with nitrate ions absorbed from soil to make amino acids, then proteins.
Exam answer essentials
  • For growth, make a causal link: more photosynthesis → more glucose → more cellulose / amino acids / proteins and respiration.
  • Do not say “glucose turns directly into protein” without the nitrate ions and amino acids link.

6. Nitrates, magnesium & plant growth

IonNeeded forDeficiency → consequence
NitrateAmino acids and therefore proteins.Fewer proteins made → stunted growth.
MagnesiumChlorophyll.Less chlorophyll → yellow leaves (chlorosis) → less light absorption → lower photosynthesis rate.
  • Plants absorb mineral ions from the soil through their roots. Mineral ions are not their energy source.
  • Root damage can reduce water and mineral uptake. Less water lowers photosynthesis; fewer nitrates limit protein synthesis; less magnesium limits chlorophyll production.
  • A mineral deficiency is different from a pathogen infection, even when both cause poor growth. The detailed comparison diagram is in Chapter 3.
Exam answer essentials
  • A connected explanation earns more than a list: fewer magnesium ions → less chlorophyll → less photosynthesis → less glucose → reduced growth. (2022 Higher Q04.3.)

7. Experiments: light, chlorophyll & carbon dioxide

RequirementComparison after destarching and exposure to lightInterpretation after the starch test
LightCover part of a leaf with opaque material; leave another part exposed.Exposed area has starch; covered area does not → light is needed.
ChlorophyllUse a variegated leaf and record the green and non-green areas before removing pigment.Green areas make starch; non-green areas do not → chlorophyll is needed.
Carbon dioxideCompare similar plants in sealed transparent containers: CO₂ removed in one, available in the control. Keep light and temperature similar.The plant with CO₂ makes starch; the one without it does not → CO₂ is needed.
  1. Boil the leaf in water to kill cells and stop reactions.
  2. Heat it in ethanol in a water bath to remove chlorophyll. The leaf loses its green colour, making the iodine result easier to see.
  3. Rinse in water to soften the leaf, then spread it on a white tile.
  4. Add iodine solution. Blue-black = starch present; orange-brown = no starch detected. Compare the correct areas or plants.
Exam answer essentials
  • The test detects starch, not chlorophyll or glucose. Record green areas before ethanol removes the colour.
  • Ethanol is flammable: use a water bath and keep it away from naked flames. Hot liquids can burn; CO₂-absorbing chemicals may be corrosive and require appropriate supervised handling.

8. Required practical 6: light intensity & pondweed

Investigate how light intensity affects photosynthesis using an aquatic organism such as pondweed. Oxygen production is used to estimate the rate.
  1. Put a freshly cut piece of pondweed in a beaker of water / sodium hydrogencarbonate solution. Hydrogencarbonate supplies carbon dioxide for photosynthesis.
  2. Place a lamp at a measured distance from the pondweed, using a ruler. Use a range of distances, for example 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 cm if suitable for the apparatus.
  3. Allow the pondweed to adjust to the conditions before measuring. Keep temperature constant using a suitable water bath / heat shield and monitor it with a thermometer.
  4. Measure oxygen produced over a fixed time. Count bubbles as a simple estimate, or collect gas using a funnel and a water-filled inverted measuring cylinder / suitable gas apparatus.
  5. Move the lamp to the next distance and repeat, keeping the same pondweed, CO₂ supply, measurement time and temperature.
  6. Repeat each condition at least three times, identify anomalies with evidence and calculate a mean. Calculate a rate and plot the results.
Variable / issueWhat to state
IndependentLight intensity, changed by moving the lamp; distance is the directly set measurement.
DependentOxygen volume per unit time, or bubble count per unit time. State the actual measurement method.
ControlsTemperature; CO₂ concentration; same species and length of pondweed; timing and apparatus.
Why measure gas volume?Bubble sizes vary, so equal counts need not represent equal oxygen volumes. Volume measures the amount more directly.
SafetyKeep electrical equipment clear of water; lamps and hot water can burn; handle glassware carefully.
Rate = oxygen volume ÷ time
  • Example: 2.4 cm³ in 4 min → 0.60 cm³/min. Or 45 bubbles in 3 min → 15 bubbles/min. Do not give volume units for a bubble count.
  • Use distance on the x-axis if asked; for a light-intensity graph, use measured intensity or a stated relative 1/d² scale.
  • Improved precision does not remove all limitations: leaks, oxygen dissolving in water, background light and temperature changes can affect readings.
Exam answer essentials
  • A method answer needs what changes, what is measured, how controls are maintained, repeats and calculation. “Keep it fair” is not a named control.
  • A closer lamp may also heat the water. Control temperature to isolate the effect of light.

9. Exam application: leaf discs & different light colours

  • Exams may change the apparatus. This is an application example, not another required practical to memorise. Follow the method given.
  • Air is removed from leaf discs so they initially sink; otherwise trapped air could make them float before photosynthesis occurs.
  • Hydrogencarbonate provides carbon dioxide, a reactant used in photosynthesis. It is not supplied to cause osmosis.
  • Photosynthesis produces oxygen → oxygen accumulates in/around discs → their density decreases relative to the solution → they rise.
  • If the measured endpoint is time for a fixed number of discs to rise, shorter time indicates a faster rate. For a relative rate, use 1/time only when the endpoint is comparable.
  • For a colour investigation, use different coloured transparent filters between lamp and sample, with light intensity and other conditions controlled.
Exam answer essentials
  • Name the measured dependent variable, such as time for discs to rise, rather than replacing it with an unmeasured “rate”. (2023 Higher Q06.4.)
  • For light-colour data: chlorophyll absorbs that wavelength → photosynthesis rate changes → oxygen production changes → rise time changes. Quote relevant data from both sources when requested. (Q06.9.)

10. Plants respire in light and darkness

ConditionsProcesses occurringNet gas exchange
DarknessAerobic respiration continues under normal oxygenated conditions; no photosynthesis without light.Oxygen taken in and CO₂ released.
Light: photosynthesis faster than respirationBoth occur.Overall CO₂ uptake and oxygen release.
Light: equal ratesBoth occur at equal rates.No net gas exchange, although both processes continue.
  • A plant does not stop respiring in daylight. Net measurements represent the balance of photosynthesis and respiration.
  • A snail with pondweed releases CO₂ by respiration; pondweed may use it in photosynthesis when light is available.
  • In darkness, both snail and pondweed respire and release CO₂. Explain a supplied indicator result using that balance.
Exam answer essentials
  • “No net change” does not mean no reactions. Describe CO₂ production by respiration and use by photosynthesis. (2021 Higher Q04.6–7.)
