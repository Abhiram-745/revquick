AQA 4.2.2.5

1. Health, disease & interacting conditions

Health is a state of physical and mental well-being. Non-communicable diseases cannot be passed from one person to another.
  • Both communicable and non-communicable diseases cause ill health. Diet, stress and life situations can also affect physical or mental health.
  • Different conditions can interact: a defective immune system increases vulnerability to infectious disease; some viruses can trigger cancers.
  • Immune reactions initially caused by a pathogen can trigger allergies, such as skin rashes or asthma. Severe physical illness can affect mental health.
  • A disease being non-communicable does not mean its risk is unrelated to infection: a virus can increase the risk of a cancer, while the cancer itself is not passed between people.
Exam answer essentials
  • For an interaction, state the first condition and how it increases risk or affects the second.

2. Risk factors: association is not proof

A risk factor is something associated with an increased chance of developing a disease. Risk factors can relate to lifestyle or substances in the body or environment.
Term / taskWhat it means for an answer
CorrelationTwo variables are associated. This alone does not establish that one causes the other.
Causal mechanismAn explanation of how the factor leads to the outcome, supported by evidence.
Confounding factorAnother variable, such as age or another lifestyle factor, may affect the outcome.
Multiple factorsSeveral risks may interact; one factor alone may not explain every case.
  • In some cases, a causal mechanism is established: examples below include smoking and cancer, alcohol and liver damage, and obesity and type 2 diabetes.
  • For a graph, identify axes and units; describe the trend with quoted data. Compare similar groups and consider sample size, follow-up time and other variables.
  • Calculate percentage change as (new − original) ÷ original × 100. Keep absolute numbers separate from percentages.
  • Disease has financial and human costs: treatment, reduced ability to work, reduced quality of life and effects on families. These costs can affect individuals, communities, nations and the world.
Exam answer essentials
  • An association does not guarantee an individual will develop the disease. Avoid claiming causation from a single correlation.

3. Cancer: benign, malignant & secondary tumours

Cancer results from changes in cells that lead to uncontrolled growth and division.
  • Benign tumours: abnormal cells grow in one area. They are usually contained and do not invade other parts of the body. They can still cause harm, for example by pressing on tissues.
  • Malignant tumours: cancer cells invade neighbouring tissues and may spread to other parts of the body.
  • Cells can enter the blood and travel elsewhere, then divide to form a secondary tumour.
  • Lifestyle risk factors include smoking and exposure to carcinogens. Genetic factors can also increase cancer risk. A higher risk is not a guarantee of cancer.
Exam answer essentials
  • To distinguish tumours, focus on invasion and spread. “Malignant is bigger” is not the defining difference.
  • A secondary tumour forms when cells spread and divide at another site; do not describe an entire tumour travelling through a vessel.
AQA 4.2.2.6

4. Smoking: explain three different mechanisms

  • Carbon monoxide binds to haemoglobin in red blood cells, reducing oxygen transport. Less oxygen reaches cells for aerobic respiration; less energy can be released for activities such as muscle contraction.
  • Smoke damages cilia → less mucus is cleared → mucus and pathogens accumulate → increased risk of respiratory problems. Lung damage reduces effective gas exchange.
  • Carcinogens in tobacco smoke damage DNA, increasing the risk of uncontrolled cell division and lung cancer.
  • Smoking increases the risk of lung disease and cardiovascular disease. Keep each mechanism linked to the outcome the question asks about.
  • Smoking during pregnancy can reduce oxygen reaching the unborn baby, affecting growth.
Exam answer essentials
  • For oxygen transport, name carbon monoxide + haemoglobin. For cancer, name carcinogens / DNA damage. Do not use the same mechanism for every smoking question.

5. Diet, exercise & type 2 diabetes

  • An unhealthy diet and lack of exercise can increase cardiovascular disease risk. High saturated-fat intake can contribute to raised blood cholesterol and fatty deposits in coronary arteries.
  • Fatty deposits narrow a coronary artery → less blood and oxygen reach heart muscle → less aerobic respiration. Severe blockage can cause a heart attack. See the coronary heart disease diagram.
  • Obesity is a risk factor for type 2 diabetes: body cells become less responsive to insulin, making blood glucose harder to control.
  • Regular activity uses energy and can reduce cardiovascular risk. Excess energy intake relative to use can lead to increased body mass. Neither body mass nor disease risk is explained by one factor alone.
  • Type 2 diabetes differs from type 1, where the pancreas fails to produce sufficient insulin. Detailed blood-glucose control is covered in the later homeostasis chapter.
Exam answer essentials
  • Write cells respond less effectively to insulin for type 2. Do not automatically describe it as “no insulin made”.
  • For coronary arteries, link narrowing to reduced oxygen supply to heart muscle, rather than just “less blood around the body”.

6. Alcohol, unborn babies & other carcinogens

Risk factorExplanation you should know
Alcohol and the liverExcessive alcohol consumption can damage liver cells and lead to liver disease.
Alcohol and the brainAlcohol affects brain function, including judgement and coordination; prolonged excessive consumption can cause damage.
Alcohol in pregnancyAlcohol can reach the developing baby and harm development.
Smoking in pregnancyReduced oxygen supply can affect the unborn baby’s growth.
CarcinogensSubstances / agents associated with increased cancer risk; examples include chemicals in tobacco smoke and ionising radiation.
  • A risk answer should name the exposure, affected organ or tissue, and resulting harm. Use details and doses given in the question.
  • Some effects have a known causal mechanism; for a new dataset, still evaluate how well it supports the claim.
  • Do not treat every non-communicable disease as caused by lifestyle: genetic and environmental factors can also matter.
Exam answer essentials
  • A good evaluation weighs evidence, benefits and risks. Write a supported conclusion rather than a moral judgement about people’s behaviour.
